## The Idea
Inspired by John Whitby Allen's [Timesaver shunting
puzzle](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timesaver), I developed a concept for a
relatively small shunting game suitable for my living room. I have an ideal,
compact space on the wall for two segment boxes, each measuring approximately
120 cm by 32 cm, which can be stored vertically one above the other. For
operation, one box will be taken down and connected to the other to create the
layout ([see below](#my-design)).

As a member of [FREMO](https://www.fremo-net.eu/), I would like to use this
model as a module at FREMO event as well. To that end, I have designed an FREMO
module adapter ([see below](#fremo-module-adapter)). For this reason, the model
also features a through track to which it is connected. 

The model recreates an industrial trapped siding – German
»Ausweichanschlussstelle (Awanst)« – within the Hamburger Hafenbahn network. But
see for yourself...

## The Subject
My shunting game, which I will call »Rubin Mill«, is inspired by the Aurora
Mill (formerly [Diamantmehl](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamantmehl) Mill).
This mill is located on the [Rethe](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rethe), a
connecting waterway in the Port of Hamburg, in
[Hamburg-Wilhelmsburg](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Wilhelmsburg), and
features quay facilities.

Several parts of the complex are listed buildings (historically preserved),
which makes them attractive to replicate, including the main building
(1953/1954), the boiler house (1896/1922), a residential building (1904), and
two of five silos (silo II and silo III, 1934). See [my
photos](#pictures-of-the-prototype) below.

The mill has a well-developed quay facility, via which it is supplied with grain
by inland waterway vessels. However, the mill also receives grain by lorry. A
2-metre-high concrete wall above the sheet piling protects the mill from
flooding. 

However, there is no longer a railway connection, and only minimal
traces of it remain – approximately 20 metres of old grooved track in the yard
entrance. The railway siding was never very extensive. In a track plan for the
[Wilhelmsburg Industrie
Bahn](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmsburger_Industriebahn) dating from
1929, I can see a small track layout with three tracks. The model is therefore,
just like its name, purely imaginary.

### My design
The design calls for two trapezoidal segments, with the front side concave (in
Hamburg Low German so called the »Waterkant«) and the back side convex for
aesthetic reasons. The tallest structure will be the chimney at 40 cm, followed
by the mill tower at 34 cm, the silos at 30 cm and the main building at 19 cm.

Track 1b has a special feature: it runs beneath the main building, which is
therefore mounted on stilts at the rear (brilliant idea by Jens).

### Period

I am planning to build the module in a way that allows it to be used with a
variety of rolling stock from Period III to VI. Today, the grain silos in the
Port of Hamburg are served by rail using containers (potentially special bulk
containers) on flat wagons. With this design, we can recreate that type of
operation as well.

### The Track Layout

The track plan is my own free interpretation, inspired by the Timesaver.
Construction features a minimum prototypical radius of 190&nbsp;m
(≙&nbsp;~119&nbsp;cm) with Code&nbsp;40 N scale track (FS160) and the points
replicate the prototype turnout geometry. The industrial site is a trapped
siding – German »Ausweichanschlussstelle (Awanst)« – to an imaginary single track
through-line of the Hamburger Hafenbahn network.

## Operations

Keep in mind, the model is not optimized for operational throughput, as it is
intended to be a shunting puzzle. However, unlike the original Timesaver, the
track lengths are dimensioned in such a way that I believe meaningful operation
is possible even at a FREMO meeting. To increase the difficulty level during
shunting puzzles, the longer tracks can simply be occupied with wagons so that
the available space becomes tight – exactly as the original Timesaver intends.

### Goods dispatch
- Flour in bulk (for large bakeries), Ucs dust container wagon pneumatic
  loading
- Flour and bran in sacks, covered goods wagons (Gs)
- Mill products for export (via quay) on pallets: sliding wall wagons (Hs)
- Direct transfer: If the mill's silos are full or the grain is not to be
  processed in this mill but sent on to another facility (e.g. inland), it is
  loaded directly from the crane into the waiting hopper wagons (Tdgs) at the
  quay

### Goods receipt
- Grain (wheat, rye, maize) mainly by coastal motor vessel and barges at the quay (elevator)
- Additives (baking agents, vitamins), pallets and sacks: covered goods wagons
  (Gs)
- Grain (wheat, rye, corn) by self-unloading wagons with swivel roofs (Ktmm,
  Tdgs)

### Operating resources
- Packaging material: sacks, pallets, cardboard boxes, covered goods wagons
  (Gs)
- Machine parts / maintenance: Flat wagons with stakes (Ks) for larger plant components
  or crates
- Heavy fuel oil (for own power station system): Tank wagon (Z)

Grain and flour could also be handled in bulk containers on container wagons and
bogie flat wagons (Lgs and Sgs).

## FREMO Module Adapter
An important part of the concept is creating an adapter to expand the shunting
game into a single-track FREMO module. This depicts an industrial spur off a
through line, spanning a total length of roughly 2.4 m at a 22.5° angle.

I am a bit concerned about achieving the necessary precision for the horizontal
curved vertical contact surface, as depicted in the drawing. Nevertheless, I
find the concept of the curved track's edge aligning with the segments appealing
(referencing the Jigsaw approach, Iain Rice, *Layout Design*, 2010, page 50).

The two segments already include the continuous track. The FREMO module adapter
is therefore purely a scenery module.

Essentially, the adapter is only used to create the FREMO module head geometry
of 400 mm x 100 mm. The FREMO module adapter will be 100 mm high, and the
segment boxes will be about 70 mm or so high, so that they can rest on the
adapter in a corresponding recess (and, of course, be secured there). See below
for more details about the [modul box construction](#module-box-construction).

## Module Box Construction

The curved shape requires special attention during the construction of the
boxes.

In total, there are 4 boxes to be build: for the left segment (A), the right
segment (B), the left FREMO adapter (C), and the right one (D). The design
principle is the same for all of them: they are constructed using a rib frame
design.

The outer two end profiles of the segment boxes measure 215 mm x 70 mm. Together
with the FREMO adapter, they form a module end compatible with the N 90 end
profile.

The segment boxes rest in a matching cut-out in the FREMO adapter. At the rear
vertical edge, the segment end is held in place with a module connector. At the
front, it is attached to the FREMO end with a knurled screw.

<PhotoSwipeImage alt="Google maps view" src={module_connector} alt="Module Connector" caption="Modul Connector" />

import module_head from './module-head.png';

<PhotoSwipeImage src={module_head} alt="Module Head">

  Here you can see how the segment (brown) rests in a cut-out of the FREMO
  adapter (green) and is secured there (blue). The result is effectively an N 90
  end profile.

</PhotoSwipeImage>

The end profile plates are made from 15 mm multiplex plywood. 7.5 mm deep
rebates and grooves are milled into the end plate for the sides, the
longitudinal ribs, and the track bed.

import segment_head from './segment-head.png';

<PhotoSwipeImage src={segment_head} alt="Segment Head">

  As an example, the right-hand end profile (KB2) of the right segment box: Turn
  the segment profile plate over so that we can see the inside: The sides,
  longitudinal ribs, track bed, and riverbed are glued into rebates and grooves
  in the end profile plate.

</PhotoSwipeImage>

The two end profile plates of each box are connected to each other via the side
panels and two longitudinal ribs each. For the two segment boxes, one
longitudinal rib forms the quay wall and runs straight. All the others form
curves, which is why the sides and longitudinal ribs are made from flexible 2 mm
aircraft plywood. The sides and longitudinal ribs are connected by 4 cross-ribs
made of 6 mm multiplex plywood per box. The longitudinal and cross-ribs are
fitted with halved lap joints and glued together.

## Current state

Current state of the planning phase.

### Open Topics / Questions about my layout and design
- Is the [operational concept](#operations) coherent and sufficiently realistic
  for an industrial mill?
- What are the practical dimensions and design for the main building's loading
  dock (s. »Laderampe« in [my design](#my-design)) At the moment, it's just
  drawn out of the blue.
- What is the absolute minimum distance I must keep between buildings and the
  track in [my design](#my-design) – particularly on the inside of the 190m
  curves? The longest wagons I plan to run are container flat wagons. 

### Module / Segment Box Construction
- My plan is to build the module and segment boxes using a rib frame
  construction.  Is this structure sufficiently rigid, particularly with regard
  to torsion?
- For the centre connection between the two segment boxes and FREMO adapter
  boxes, I have currently planned two module connectors as a alignment fit and
  two wing screws each. Is there a better option, particularly one that allows
  the boxes to be connected quickly and easily by oneself without any assistance
  needed?
- How do I assemble the transport boxes?
- How do I assemble the modular legs? How could the transport boxes be used as
  legs?
- How do I transfer the [track layout plan](#the-track-layout) onto the track
  board? Printing it out on paper and sticking it on causes the paper to expand
  due to moisture, meaning the dimensions no longer match up perfectly. Don't
  know if this will be a problem.
- How will I control and operate the turnouts? Do I need to make provision for
  this now, whilst the module is being built?  For example, to ensure there is
  sufficient space between the ribs.
